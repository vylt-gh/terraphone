Terraphone is an interactive scientific experience created for the NASA Space Apps Challenge. Earth is constantly making music — our mission is to explore how our planet sounds, how planetary rainfall patterns unfold, and how human urban-planning decisions change our acoustic soundscape.

🌧️ Experience 1: Global Rain

Explore worldwide precipitation data from NASA's Global Precipitation Measurement (GPM) constellation draped over a tactile 3D bumpy globe. Live precipitation data (NASA SVS Entry 5650) drives procedural rain droplet physics, convective NASA thunder rumbles, and location-dependent habitat crossfading (urban, rural, rainforest, ocean).

🏙️ Experience 2: The Sound of Change

Listen to a Manhattan street, try 20 planning interventions, and compare the simulated noise index and live 64-band spectrum.

  • Transportation & Traffic: Electric transit, low-noise asphalt, speed management, and anti-honking cameras.
  • Architecture & Sound Insulation: STC-45 building insulation (primarily protects indoor spaces), acoustic facade design, noise barriers, and green buildings.
  • Urban Planning & Source Control: Heavy industry relocation, linear green belts, entertainment inspections, and construction curfews.
  • Public Awareness & Health: Community quiet hours, anti-honking culture, and responsible headphone habits (protects personal hearing health).

🎮 Experience 3: Earth Arcade

Six environmental exploration games where NASA and NOAA satellite data directly shape procedural worlds: Fireline (2.5D isometric forest exploration with NASA FIRMS active fire alerts), Heat Escape (five-lane runner inspired by NASA ECOSTRESS surface temperatures), Cloud Courier (NASA OMI SO₂/NO₂), Ozone Drift (NASA Ozone Watch), Coral Current (NOAA Coral Reef Watch), and Last Forest (NASA/USGS Landsat). Earn Zen tokens to unlock custom cosmetics and spirit glows!

📐 Scientifically Responsible Modeling

Sound levels combine logarithmically rather than linearly: L_total = 10 · log10( Σ 10^(Li / 10) ) A 3 dB decrease represents a 50% cut in acoustic sound energy. The simulation avoids double-counting overlapping interventions and provides both Outdoor (sidewalk) and Indoor (apartment) acoustic perspectives.
